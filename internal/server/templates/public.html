Security & data

{{T "public.about_unofficial" . (slots "how" (link "/features" (noboost)))}}

The short version

p.stonn signs in to your council account and changes which rego is on your visitor permit, following the schedule you set. To do that it keeps a live connection to your account. p.stonn encrypts that connection on the server, and your council password too if you choose to save it.

You stay in control: disconnect any time in Settings and it takes effect immediately, and “Delete all my data” in Settings removes everything you’ve stored. The one thing to do at your end is use a council password you don’t use anywhere else, so nothing else of yours is exposed in the unlikely event this server is ever broken into.

What’s encrypted, and what isn’t

p.stonn encrypts these with AES-256-GCM: your council session, its access token, your saved password, and the codes behind your printed and visitor QR links.

p.stonn stores these as plain text: your number plates, your schedule, your permit details, email addresses, and the activity and change logs. None of that is a login to your council account, but anyone with access to this server or its database could read it.

How the connection ends

The connection lasts while the account is in use and stops after 90 days with nobody signing in (anyone you share the account with counts). About a week before, p.stonn emails a one-click link to keep it going. This is deliberate: p.stonn should not hold access to the council account of someone who has moved away or stopped using it.

You can also end the connection yourself any time. Disconnecting in Settings ends it immediately. Changing your council password ends the stored session (an access token already issued may keep working for up to about an hour).

Your council password, and signing in

p.stonn saves your password by default, encrypted, so it can sign back in on its own when the council ends a session. Without it, a lapsed session stops your schedule until you reconnect by hand. Untick “Save my password” when you link, or turn it off later in Settings, and p.stonn erases it and never stores it again.

Your p.stonn sign-in is separate: p.stonn sends a one-time code to your verified email. You can only ever link the council account whose email matches it.

What data p.stonn holds

About you: your email, your regos and their labels, your schedule, your permit details, a log of the changes p.stonn has made, a log of who on the account changed what, your notification settings, and the encrypted council session (plus your password, unless you turned that off).

About other people, only where you have used those features: the email addresses of anyone you gave shared access to or sent a guest pass to, any email you attached to a rego, and the number plates visitors typed at a printed QR. If an address bounces or its owner reports our email as spam, p.stonn adds it to a do-not-email list and stops trying it.

How long it is kept

p.stonn keeps activity and change logs for 90 days, and a visitor’s number plate from a printed QR for 7 days after the scan. A revoked guest pass stops naming its recipient after 30 days. p.stonn deletes the content of each notification as soon as it has gone out. The server’s own operational logs hold number plates and permit IDs, plus a redacted form of email addresses (the first letter and the domain). Those logs are limited in size and age, so they cover about the last couple of weeks. On the do-not-email list, p.stonn removes a bounce or an unsubscribe after two years, but keeps a spam complaint so that a request to stop never quietly lapses.

How your data is used

p.stonn uses your data only to run the service for you. We never sell it or use it for advertising. The app has no trackers or analytics, and your browser makes no requests to any third party. p.stonn shares data only where a feature needs it: with the email provider that delivers your notifications, with the push service if you turn push notifications on, and with the council when p.stonn changes your permit.

The server administrator can technically see what is stored on the server, as with any self-hosted tool. p.stonn is small and personal, and we use your data only to run the service for you. We never sell it, never share it beyond what a feature needs, and never look at it for anything else.

Deleting your data

“Delete all my data” in Settings clears everything from the live database straight away: your session, saved password, schedule, regos, guest passes, logs, and anything queued to be emailed to you. It also removes your traces from any account you were a member of, including guest passes you created there.

Backups are the exception. p.stonn takes an encrypted backup daily and thins them out over time (roughly the last week daily, the last month weekly, then monthly for about three months), so deleted data can linger in a backup for up to about three months before it is gone for good. Backups are not reachable through the app. We hold the key that encrypts them separately from the server.

If the server were compromised

If this server or its encryption key were ever exposed, someone could use your saved council session the way you can, to view what is held in your {{.Tenant.Terms.portal}} account and manage the permits in it. Changing your {{.Tenant.Terms.portal}} password shuts the session out immediately. Encryption makes that exposure unlikely. Using a password that is unique to your council account keeps any fallout contained there. As with any online service, none of this is an absolute guarantee.

Notifications

p.stonn tries to tell you, by email or push, each time it changes your permit or can’t. This is best-effort: messages can be delayed, filtered, or lost, and the service can be down. You remain responsible for your permit and for any fines.

{{T "public.for_council_heading" .}}

If you work for the council and have questions about this service, please get in touch{{if .Contact}} through the contact form{{end}}. We would be glad to talk. p.stonn acts only with each resident’s own login, on their own permit, and only when that resident’s schedule needs a change. It signs in the same way the {{.Tenant.Terms.portal}} website does, then uses the council’s own permit API to change the rego, and it identifies itself on those requests as p.stonn, with a link back to this site. p.stonn keeps its load light. A single change of rego takes three requests (reading the current rego, changing it and reading it back to confirm), which is fewer than a resident makes doing the same by hand in a browser. Between changes, p.stonn contacts the council only a handful of times a day: one request roughly every eight hours to keep the session open, and a permit read twice a day to stay in step. That comes to about a dozen requests across a whole day for one household, spaced out and slowing down if the portal is busy. If the council offers permit scheduling itself, or an official way for residents to delegate access, p.stonn will either adopt it or close.

No promises

p.stonn is provided as-is, with no warranty of any kind. It may stop working, make mistakes, or lose data. Use it at your own risk.

What p.stonn can do

{{T "public.history" .}}

The old paper permits could be moved between cars as needed. The digital permit holds one rego at a time, so a household that relies on regular help, such as grandparents doing the childcare pickup, a nanny or a carer, has to sign in and change the rego each time a different person visits. That quickly becomes a recurring chore.

{{T "public.attempt" .}}

We built p.stonn because we got tired of signing in to swap the rego every time our parents came to help with the kids. You’re welcome to use it, too.

{{end}}
{{if not .SignedIn}}

Connect once

Sign in to p.stonn with the same email address your {{.Tenant.Terms.portal}} account uses, because p.stonn can only connect to the council account that matches. Then link once with your council password: p.stonn keeps an encrypted connection so it can update your permit on your schedule, and you don’t sign in again each time.

{{end}}

A rego for each day

Set which rego is on the permit for each day of the week, such as the nanny on weekdays and grandparents on the weekend. If your helpers alternate, the roster can run over a fortnight or four weeks and rotate through them. p.stonn keeps the council permit in sync, so you’re not signing in every time a different person visits.

{{template "demo-roster" .}}

One-off bookings

When something breaks the routine, such as a weekend visitor, an overnight stay or a holiday, make a booking for a set window. Your roster takes back over the moment it ends.

{{template "demo-oneoff" .}}

Add a visitor with a QR

Tap Show visitor QR and hold up your phone. Your visitor scans it and types their number plate, and p.stonn puts it on the permit until the end of the day. They don’t need an account or anything installed.

The code stops working after 15 minutes, so it only ever works for the person standing in front of you.

{{template "demo-visitorqr" .}}

A printed QR you confirm on your phone

Print a QR code once and stick it on the fridge or by the front door, wherever your visitors will see it. It keeps working until you remove it, and you confirm each scan on your phone: you get a notification with the visitor’s rego, and p.stonn changes nothing until you say yes.

{{template "demo-doorqr" .}}

A pass for regular visitors

For the people who come every week, such as the nanny or your parents, send a guest pass. They keep the link; when they arrive they tap their rego and it is on the permit until the end of the day. p.stonn tells you each time.

A pass works whenever they choose, until you delete it, so only send it to people you trust.

{{template "demo-guestpass" .}}

One tap from your own home screen

Save the quick picker to your phone and every rego in the household becomes a button. Tap one and it is on the permit until the end of the day, with no sign-in, exactly as a booking made in the app would be.

The quick picker offers every rego you have, so one you add later appears on it by itself. If the phone is lost, a new link stops the old one working.

{{template "demo-picker" .}}

Know when your permit changes

p.stonn emails or notifies you whenever it changes your permit, or when it can’t. Delivery isn’t guaranteed, so you stay responsible for your permit, but you’ll usually know the moment anything changes.

{{T "public.before_intro" .}}

If you are not set up yet:

  • Check you are eligible. Permits are for residents in permit areas, and eligibility depends on your property (generally limited or no off-street parking).
  • Apply through the council. You can apply online, by email, by post, or in person at a customer service centre. You will usually need proof you live at the address and your vehicle details.
  • Set up your online account. You manage your permits and enter regos in the council’s online system.
  • Add a visitor permit. Once your account exists, apply for a visitor permit if you do not already have one. Fees apply, with a discount for concession cardholders.
  • Note the email address your {{.Tenant.Terms.portal}} account uses. You’ll sign in to p.stonn with that same address, which is how p.stonn knows which council account is yours.

The council's own pages have the current fees, forms and details:

  • Parking permits
  • Visitor parking permits
  • Digital parking permit FAQs

Once your council account has a visitor permit, sign in to p.stonn with that account’s email address and connect it.
